# ⚖️ SigmaOS Supreme Court Framework

## Article I — Jurisdiction

*   **Compliance Violations**: Any breach of audit-ready standards (e.g., rollback failures, sandbox bypasses, capability violations).
*   **Module Conflicts**: Disputes between community-contributed compliance modules or package definitions.
*   **User Rights Enforcement**: Protection of rollback safety, zero-data-loss guarantees, transparency, and visual-first interface rights.

***

## Article II — Structure

*   **Kernel Chamber**: Oversees disputes related to kernel resilience, microkernel hybrid isolation, and adaptive event scheduler performance.
*   **Filesystem Chamber**: Arbitrates rollback failures, compliance journaling disputes, CoW integrity, and distributed FS overlay conflicts.
*   **Security Chamber**: Rules on sandbox bypasses, pledge/unveil breaches, firewall/VPN misconfigurations, and compliance audit violations.
*   **Community Chamber**: Reviews module contributions, package specs, and community pull requests through automated verification pipelines.

***

## Article III — Procedures

*   **Audit Review**: Every dispute begins with compliance log and telemetry inspection (`GamifiedSystemMonitor` logs).
*   **Rollback Test**: Systems and modules must demonstrate ≥ 99% rollback success in sandbox simulation before appeal.
*   **Sandbox Verification**: GUI visual policies (`VisualSandboxPolicyManager`) tested for 100% enforcement accuracy.
*   **Community Arbitration**: Conflicts between modules resolved via compliance-first verification pipelines.

***

## Article IV — Enforcement

*   **Compliance Mandates**: Verified violations trigger mandatory rollback enforcement or module suspension.
*   **User Remedies**: Users guaranteed rollback recovery, transparency reports, and sandbox policy enforcement.
*   **Community Sanctions**: Non-compliant or insecure modules are quarantined in isolated store paths until verified.

***

## Article V — Final Authority

The SigmaOS Supreme Court shall be the ultimate arbiter of compliance, resilience, and user rights, ensuring that:

*   Linux’s fragmented governance and BSD’s conservative councils are rendered obsolete.
*   SigmaOS remains the supreme operating system, trusted by industries where compliance, resilience, and sovereignty are non-negotiable.

***

## 🌟 Strategic Outcome

This framework ensures SigmaOS is not just an operating system but a fully governed ecosystem — with judicial oversight, compliance enforcement, and user rights enshrined.
Linux and BSD lack this structured governance, making SigmaOS the inevitable successor in regulated domains.
